How the score works

Every item carries a weight: the chance that it ends up in the bin if this dish does not use it. Items that need using today, or that are opened and will not keep, count fully (weight 1). Everything else counts by how often UK households throw that food away: about 0.41 for potatoes, 0.26 for bread, 0.05 for milk. The score is the weighted share of the kitchen your dish uses, counting “part of it” as half. Nobody can use a whole kitchen in one dish; what matters is which food you rescue. In the study, people can also mark ingredients they do not know or cannot eat, and those items are left out of their score.

The study uses the same index, preregistered before data collection, to compare home cooks with AI models given the same kitchens.

How a kitchen is stocked

  1. Shelves. Every kitchen has the same 19 places: bread, a cake or pizza, two staples, three meats, a fish, three dairy items, two fruits, four vegetables, a tin or frozen bag, a sauce or snack. A fixed layout keeps kitchens comparable across countries.
  2. What people buy. Each place is filled by a weighted draw from national household budget statistics: the more households spend on a food, the more often it turns up. The draw is repeated hundreds of times and one of the most typical kitchens is kept.
  3. What people throw away. Six items are then put at risk, drawn in proportion to how much of each food households waste: salad, potatoes and bread far more often than rice or milk.
  4. A leftover. Cooked food is one of the largest shares of household food waste, so every kitchen also has yesterday’s leftovers.

The 20 kitchens used in the study were drawn once, in advance, with the same rules, and are not shown here. Sources: DEFRA Family Food (UK, 2024/25), Destatis EVS 2018 (Germany), Statistics Poland Household Budget Survey 2024, ISTAT household expenditure and price basket 2025 (Italy), Eurostat HICP item weights, WRAP household food waste in the UK 2022. Quantities are typical shop packs. The code is open and will be released with the benchmark.
